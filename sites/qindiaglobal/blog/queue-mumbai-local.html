Queue or rush? What happens at the door of a Mumbai local

By Q India (Qindiaglobal) · 20 November 2026 · 6 minute read

Every morning, lakhs of us stand on a Mumbai platform and watch the local come in. In those few seconds we make a choice: push for the door, or wait our turn. This post looks at what the news says happens when we rush, and what changes when we queue.

What the news reports say

These are real reports from Indian and international news outlets. Each one has a link, so you can read it yourself. Numbers are given exactly as the reports give them.

Mumbra, 9 June 2025: 13 commuters fall from two crowded trains

Commuters standing on the footboards of two overcrowded locals passing on a curve near Mumbra were knocked off when their bags brushed. Railway police said 13 people fell; four died and nine were injured. Free Press Journal, citing railway police figures, reported that 107 passengers died after falling from moving trains in the Thane and Dombivli areas in 2024 alone.

  • The Tribune (PTI), 9 June 2025: 4 killed, 9 injured after falling off moving train in Maharashtra's Thane
  • Business Standard (PTI), 10 June 2025: 'Killer bag' brushed against passengers, causing deadly fall
  • Business Standard, 9 June 2025: 4 dead, 6 injured after passengers fall from overcrowded Mumbai local train
  • Free Press Journal, 10 June 2025: Overcrowding, AC locals and a dangerous curve; why commuters fell to their deaths

June 2025: a 21-year-old falls from the footboard, the fourth case that month

A young man from Mumbra died after falling from a crowded train between Mumbra and Kalwa. He was standing on the footboard because the coach was full. The report says it was the fourth such case that month on the same stretch.

  • Free Press Journal, 29 June 2025: 21-year-old dies after falling from overcrowded coach

Elphinstone Road, 29 September 2017: 22 die on a crowded footbridge

During the morning rush, in heavy rain, panic broke out on the narrow footbridge between Elphinstone Road and Parel stations. At least 22 people died in the stampede and more than 30 were injured. It is a reminder that crowding and pushing are dangerous on bridges and stairs too, not only at the train door.

  • Business Standard (PTI), 30 September 2017: 22 die in Mumbai foot overbridge stampede
  • NPR, 29 September 2017: Fear of bridge collapse triggers stampede in India

The big numbers

  • 6,760 deaths and 14,257 injuries from falling off trains between January 2014 and May 2025, out of 29,970 deaths on the network from all causes in 11 years (railway police data). ThePrint (PTI), 10 June 2025
  • 210 people died falling from running trains in just the first five months of 2025, out of 922 accidental deaths. Free Press Journal, 7 July 2025
  • 525 deaths from falls from moving trains in 2025, linked to overcrowding, out of 2,287 deaths in all (down 7% from 2024). Mumbai Live
  • About 10 lives a day: 3,588 people were killed on the suburban railway in 2024, according to figures before the Bombay High Court. Think Global Health, 15 December 2025
  • More than 52,300 people died on Mumbai's local train network in about 20 years, the Railways told the Bombay High Court. Scroll, 29 August 2024

Not every death on the railway is caused by rushing the door. Many happen while crossing tracks. But falls from crowded trains and doors are one of the biggest causes, and they are the ones each of us can help prevent.

When we do not queue

  • Falls and deaths. People are pushed into the gap between train and platform, or off the footboard once the train moves.
  • Injuries and fights. Elbows, bags and shoving at the door lead to cuts, broken bones and arguments every day.
  • Slower boarding. When people push in while others are trying to get off, nobody moves. The door stays blocked and the train is held up.
  • The weak are left behind. The elderly, children, pregnant women and people with disabilities cannot fight their way in, so they miss the train or get hurt.
  • Theft and harassment. A pushing crowd is where pickpockets and harassers hide.
  • Stress. Starting and ending every working day in a fight is exhausting.

When we queue

  • Safer for everyone. Nobody is pushed towards the edge or the gap.
  • Faster doors. When people get off first, the doorway clears and the line moves in quickly.
  • Fair. Whoever came first boards first. Strength does not decide who gets home.
  • Room for those who need it. Seniors, women with children and people with disabilities can board in dignity.
  • Calm journeys. Fewer fights, less theft, and a better start to the day.
  • A habit that spreads. When a few people stand in line, others follow. Queues at bus stops and metro stations show it works.

What you can do today

  1. Stand in a line on the platform, behind the yellow line.
  2. Let people get off first. Keep the middle of the door clear.
  3. Never get on or off a moving train, and do not travel on the footboard.
  4. Move inside the coach. Do not block the door.
  5. If the train is too full, wait for the next one. Your life is worth more than a few minutes.
